# TOPIC: POPULATION SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Population distribution and the factors that influence it

### Meaning of population distribution
**Population distribution** is the way people are spread across an area or country. Different parts of Uganda have different numbers of people. Some areas have many people living on a small amount of land, while others have only a few people. An area with many people compared with its size is described as **densely populated**. An area with very few people compared with its size is described as **sparsely populated**. An area whose population is well supported by its resources may be described as having **optimum population**. Population distribution helps explain settlement patterns.

### Climate, soils and relief
**Climate** influences where people settle. Areas receiving reliable rainfall support farming and tend to attract more people. Dry areas have less rainfall and may have fewer people. **Soil fertility** matters because fertile soils support crop production. **Relief** also influences settlement. Steep mountain slopes can have soil erosion, landslides and difficult transport, while gently sloping land may offer easier farming and road construction. People therefore choose settlement areas partly because of the conditions of the land and their ability to grow food.
<!-- source: lchk_14b623f1a3ce45c2ba8e608ccdc49bdb "Rainfall distribution/ climate." -->

### Jobs, towns and social services
Employment opportunities attract people towards industries, markets and towns. The source notes explain that urban areas have many opportunities to work and earn an income. People may move from rural areas to towns in search of jobs. Areas with good **social services**, including health care, education and transport, attract more people than places with poor services. Towns and trading centres may be densely settled because people can access services, transport and business opportunities. These factors help explain why Uganda's population is not evenly spread.
<!-- source: lchk_f2bbe26a485b4f19a8d163a63cee3aef "Economic opportunities lead to migration of people in search of employment" -->

### Security and government policy
People often settle in areas with peace and security and may leave places affected by wars. Government can also restrict settlement in certain areas such as game parks, reserves and swamps. Vegetation, diseases and natural disasters can further affect where people live. For example, dense forest may contain dangerous animals and disease vectors, while flooded areas may be unsuitable for housing. The location of natural resources, roads and services may increase the population of particular areas. These many influences combine to shape population distribution across the country.
