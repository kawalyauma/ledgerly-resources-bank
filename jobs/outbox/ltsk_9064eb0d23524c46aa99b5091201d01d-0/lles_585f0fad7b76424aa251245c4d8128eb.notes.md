# TOPIC: POPULATION SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Densely populated areas of Uganda

### Dense population
A **densely populated area** has many people living in it compared with the size of its land. The sources describe densely settled places as places where people can obtain food, jobs, services or other resources. Different parts of Uganda have different population levels. The number of people living around lakes, towns, riverbanks and suitable farming land may be high. Dense population should not be confused with population growth: density concerns people compared with land area, while growth describes changes in population numbers over time.

### Examples in Uganda
The learning notes give examples of densely settled areas in Uganda. These include areas near **Lake Victoria**, parts of **Kisoro** and **Kabale**, and places around **Mount Elgon**, including Mbale and Kapchorwa. Urban centres such as towns, cities and trading centres also attract many residents. The notes additionally name shores of lakes, riverbanks and plateau areas. These are examples of broad settlement patterns rather than exact numbers. Different areas may attract people for different reasons, such as fertile land, fishing, trade or services.

### Why lake shores and riverbanks attract people
Places near lakes and rivers can attract population because water is available for activities. The source notes explain that lakeshores often receive dependable rainfall and have fertile soils favourable for growing crops. Fishing provides a means of earning a living. Riverbanks may also offer fertile soil, water and farming opportunities. Some waterside settlements develop trade and transport activity. These factors can encourage residents to remain near lakes and rivers. They help explain why areas around Lake Victoria are among Uganda's relatively densely settled places in the supplied notes.
<!-- source: lchk_44a9f6816d1644cca9d39ea62fc34330 "Examples of densely populated areas." -->

### Why towns and fertile highlands attract people
**Urban areas** attract people with employment opportunities, markets, transport and social services. Industries and businesses may offer work, while nearby schools and health centres support families. Fertile farming areas may have many people because crops can be grown successfully. Places with reliable rainfall and good roads can also support trade. Some plateau areas and gently sloping land are favourable for cultivation and settlement. Where employment, food production and services are available together, more people may settle and populations may become dense.
