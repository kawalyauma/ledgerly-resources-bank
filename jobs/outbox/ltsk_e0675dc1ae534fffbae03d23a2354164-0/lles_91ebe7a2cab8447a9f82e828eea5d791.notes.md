# TOPIC: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Practice: calculating population density

### Remember
1. **Population density** is the average number of persons per square kilometre.
2. **Population** means the number of people living in an area.
3. The **area** is measured in square kilometres (km²).
4. Divide the total number of people by the number of square kilometres to find the density.
5. Give the answer as **people per square kilometre**.
6. The Cornerstone Junior School question gives 600 pupils and an area of 30 square kilometres.
7. The source gives an answer of 20 pupils per square kilometre for Cornerstone Junior School.
8. Another source includes population figures for Iganga and Matere for density calculations.

### Population and area figures
The same population can be found in places of different sizes. The source describes Iganga town with **80,000 people** living on **400 square kilometres**, resulting in **200 people per square kilometre**. It also gives Matere town council with **84,000 people** on **240 square kilometres**, producing **350 people per square kilometre**. The total number of people must be placed above the land area in the division. A total population and land area must both be known.

### Checking a density answer
In the source exercise for Town W, **4,500 people** live in **90 km²**. Dividing 4,500 by 90 gives **50 people per square kilometre**. The answer describes the average number of people in each square kilometre, not the total size of the population. The population and area numbers are taken unchanged from the teaching materials. These values give different answers because the numbers of people and land areas differ.
