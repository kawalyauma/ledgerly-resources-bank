# TOPIC: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Practice: low population density

### Remember
1. **Sparse population** is when there are very few people compared with the size of the land.
2. **Low population density** means few people compared with available resources.
3. Semi-arid areas may have few settlers because there is little rainfall and the soil may be infertile.
4. Steep mountain slopes can have few settlers because transport is difficult and farming with machines is difficult.
5. Civil wars and natural disasters can force people to leave their homes.
6. A small population may have plenty of land and low crime rates.
7. Low population may also mean a small market, few workers and low tax revenue.
8. Better rural services and industries are named as ways of attracting settlement.

### Areas with few people
Sparsely populated areas named in the resources include **semi-arid areas**, **forests**, **steep slopes**, **rural areas** and places affected by **civil wars** or **natural disasters**. Such places may have too little rainfall, poor roads, poor soils or too few opportunities for work. In forested areas, the government may restrict settlement, and dangerous animals or disease-carrying insects may also discourage settlement. These facts explain why few people live in some parts of a country.
<!-- source: lchk_0329a4dbd60a4de18ec0b22493198734 "Examples of sparsely populated areas." -->

### Reasons and effects
Rainfall, soil fertility, security, employment and services affect where people settle. Many people move towards towns where jobs and public services are available. In less populated communities, land may be sufficient but the number of workers and customers may remain low. The textbook links this situation to underuse of resources and a smaller market for goods. Improved social services and new industries may attract more residents.
