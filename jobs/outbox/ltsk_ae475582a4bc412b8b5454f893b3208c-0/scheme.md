# Social Studies Primary Five 2026 Term 3: The Government of Uganda; Population, Size and Distribution

Ten weeks, five 40-minute periods a week. Weeks 1-5 (24 lessons) cover NCDC Topic 11, The Government of Uganda: role and levels of government, the three organs, the constitution, duties of government including communication, revenue and expenditure, and rights and responsibilities of citizens including child labour. Week 5 period 5 to week 10 period 4 (25 lessons) cover NCDC Topic 12, Population, Size and Distribution: population and census, growth, distribution, density, high and low density, HIV/AIDS, control of population growth and family planning. Each subtopic is followed by a practice (Part B) lesson using exercise and past-paper questions from the pack. Week 10 period 5 is the end of term assessment.

## The Government of Uganda (weeks 1-5)

Identifies the three organs of government. Explains the meaning of the constitution, its functions and importance. Explains the duties of the government. Explains the sources of revenue and expenditure by the government. Explains the rights and responsibilities of citizens of Uganda. Assessment: explain the organs of a nation and their significance; discuss the importance of a constitution; draw a chart showing how government gets revenue and spends it.

Explains the importance of a census. Explains the influence of population growth on communities. Explains what population distribution means. Discusses problems associated with high and low population density and their solutions. Explains possible ways of controlling population growth. Assessment: write the causes of population growth; list the difficulties a family finds in looking after many children; list the problems found in a crowded community such as a slum.

## Gaps

- NCDC lists 'handles foreign affairs and sanity of the nation' as a duty of government; the notes only say the president 'represents the country internationally', so no separate lesson on foreign affairs.
- NCDC asks learners to explain the necessity of the separation of the legislature and judiciary; the notes only say the Legislature makes laws the Judiciary uses. No fuller explanation in the pack.
- NCDC expenditure items 'rent' and revenue item 'sale of government bills' are not explained in any notes in the pack.
- NCDC competence 'Write a composition "The country I want to live in"' has no guidance or model in the pack.
- NCDC meaning of teenagers and the 2007 teenager figure appear only in the curriculum; no notes expand on them.
- Population figures in the pack conflict and are dated (NCDC: 41.49 million, 2016; Excel notes: over 45 million, 2020; another notes book says the recent census was 2003). The pack has no current census figure.
- Names of office holders in the notes (speaker, chief justice, 2020 cabinet) are out of date; the pack has no current list.
- Term 3 past papers in the pack have very few Social Studies questions on these two topics (mostly budget, revenue, human rights, population and political head of a district); practice lessons rely mainly on notes exercises.
