# TOPIC: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Family planning and family size

### Meaning of family planning
**Family planning** is the spacing of births of children in a family. It is described in the supplied resource as the spaced way of producing children. Planning allows parents to prepare for the needs of their children. The source links family planning to control of rapid population growth. It lists two broad groups of methods: **natural** methods and **artificial** methods. The examples named in the source under artificial methods include condoms and injections.
<!-- source: lchk_c39ba86482504f359b37660b3e5a1d42 "Family planning is the spaced way of producing children." -->

### Advantages of family planning
The source explains that family planning helps prevent unwanted births and can control sudden population increases. It can help improve a family's financial position and allow parents to plan for their children's needs. The material also states that family planning may reduce high maternal mortality among women. In a planned family, parents can more easily organise the provision of food, shelter, school fees and other basic needs for their children.
<!-- source: lchk_c39ba86482504f359b37660b3e5a1d42 "It helps the family to plan for the children effectively." -->

### Advantages of a small family
The source says a **small family** is generally easier to plan for. Parents may find it easier to provide **basic needs**, send their children to school and give time and attention to each child. A small family may not need as much land. It can help reduce quarrels between family members. The source also mentions that children may enjoy parental love and care. These are reasons given for encouraging a manageable family size.
<!-- source: lchk_c39ba86482504f359b37660b3e5a1d42 "Children enjoy parental love" -->

### Disadvantages and concerns
The resource notes possible disadvantages associated with a small family, including a shortage of people to provide labour or security in a home. It says a very small population may reduce the labour force and tax base of a country. Its discussion of family planning also notes concerns about reduced population growth. These points are presented alongside the advantages; families need to consider their ability to care for children and meet basic needs.
