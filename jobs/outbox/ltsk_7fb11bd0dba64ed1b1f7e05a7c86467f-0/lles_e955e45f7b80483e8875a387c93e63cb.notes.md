# TOPIC: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Practice: controlling population growth and family planning

### Remember
1. **Population growth** is an increase in the number of people in an area.
2. **Family planning** means spacing the births of children.
3. The sources list natural and artificial methods of family planning.
4. Family planning can help families provide children's basic needs.
5. Girls' education and discouraging early marriage are named as ways to control rapid population growth.
6. **Unplanned family size** and early marriages can contribute to fast growth.
7. Rapid population growth may bring food shortages, land fragmentation, unemployment and pressure on services.
8. The source also suggests providing more employment, food and social services in growing communities.

### Family needs and population growth
A large family may find it difficult to provide food, enough land, housing, schooling and other basic needs when resources are limited. The resources connect rapid population growth with shortages of food and land, crowded settlement and insufficient public services. They also describe unemployment and difficulties in protecting the environment. Parents and governments therefore need to plan for the number of children and people who need services.
<!-- source: lchk_401b8346cdcf44109b333a534b4a6b3d "It leads to shortage of land for settlement leading to land fragmentation." -->

### Ways of managing growth
The supplied resources name **family planning campaigns**, **girls' education** and discouraging **early marriages** and **polygamy** as measures for limiting fast population growth. They also mention educating families about the cost of raising many children. Modernised agriculture can help provide food, while industries may offer more employment. These are both growth-control and development measures described in the resource pack.
