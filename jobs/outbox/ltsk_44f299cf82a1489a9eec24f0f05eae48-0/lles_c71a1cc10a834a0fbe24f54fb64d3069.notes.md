# TOPIC: THE GOVERNMENT OF UGANDA
## SUB-TOPIC: The Judiciary and the courts of law

### Meaning and composition of the Judiciary
The **Judiciary** is the organ of government responsible for interpreting laws and settling cases in courts of law. It is sometimes described as the custodian of the law. The Judiciary is headed by the **Chief Justice**. The supplied lesson notes identify judges, justices, magistrates, attorneys and lawyers as people who make up the Judiciary. These officers work with laws made by the Legislature. The Judiciary is different from Parliament, which makes laws, and the Executive, which administers government programmes.

### Duties of the Judiciary
The Judiciary performs several important duties.
1. It interprets laws to the people.
2. It settles disagreements and disputes in courts of law.
3. It decides whether laws have been broken.
4. It punishes people found guilty of breaking the law.
5. It protects the rights of citizens through fair court trials.
Law breakers may be punished through fines or imprisonment. By settling cases, courts help people use legal procedures to resolve disputes. Courts are places where cases are heard and decisions are made under the law.
<!-- source: lchk_f1a2ace4ed3242358337e728ce8ef8cb "Duties of the judiciary." -->

### The Chief Justice
The **Chief Justice** is the head of the Judiciary and the Supreme Court. The Chief Justice guides judges and magistrates as they perform their duties. The Chief Justice also presides over the swearing-in of important political leaders, including the President. The supplied notes say the Chief Justice can discipline judicial officers who are out of order. This office has responsibilities for leading the courts and ensuring that judicial duties are carried out. The Chief Justice is assisted by other senior judicial officers.
<!-- source: lchk_f1a2ace4ed3242358337e728ce8ef8cb "Roles of the Chief justice." -->

### Courts of law and their heads
The courts named in the supplied P5 learning notes include **Resident Magistrate's Court**, headed by a magistrate; **Chief Magistrate's Court**, headed by a chief magistrate; **High Court**, headed by the Principal Judge; **Court of Appeal**, headed by the Deputy Chief Justice; and **Supreme Court**, headed by the Chief Justice. The Supreme Court is the highest court and final court of appeal. The Court of Appeal hears appeals from the High Court. The High Court also hears appeals from courts below it. These courts form different levels of hearing and appealing cases.
