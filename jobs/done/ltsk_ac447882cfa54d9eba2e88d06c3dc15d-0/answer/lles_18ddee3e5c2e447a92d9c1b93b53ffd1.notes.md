# TOPIC: THE GOVERNMENT OF UGANDA
## SUB-TOPIC: Organs of government: the Executive

### Three organs of government
The government has three **organs** or arms. They are the **Executive**, **Legislature** and **Judiciary**. Each organ has important but different work. The Legislature makes laws for people to obey and respect. The Judiciary interprets laws made by the Legislature. The Executive administers government decisions and programmes. These organs are all parts of the government, but they are not the same institution and do not carry out exactly the same duties.

### Meaning of the Executive
The **Executive** is the main ruling and administrative organ of government. Its task is to carry out the daily work of administering the country. The Executive is concerned with planning for the country and making sure government policies and programmes are implemented. It manages the national affairs and works to keep peace and order. The Executive is different from the Legislature, whose main work is making laws, and the Judiciary, whose work is interpreting laws.
<!-- source: lchk_34af8a516be44e0ba5c1a92a22a56fc8 "It is the main administrative organ of the government." -->

### Members of the Executive
The **President** heads the Executive. The organ is made up of the President, ministers and civil servants. The President leads national administration and chairs Cabinet meetings. Ministers are the political heads of ministries and make sure their ministries carry out government policies. Civil servants work through ministries and other government offices to put public programmes into action. Together these people form the Executive organ. Their work is connected because programmes need leadership and everyday administration.
<!-- source: lchk_34af8a516be44e0ba5c1a92a22a56fc8 "This organ is made up of the president, ministers and civil servants." -->

### Duties of the Executive
The Executive has many responsibilities in Uganda.
1. It plans for and rules the country.
2. It implements government policies and programmes.
3. It maintains law and order in the country.
4. It collects and spends government revenue.
5. It promotes development and peace.
All these tasks concern administering the country. Government programmes such as education, health and roads must be carried out by the responsible offices. Learners should distinguish the Executive's administrative work from Parliament's law-making duty and the courts' work of interpreting the law.
