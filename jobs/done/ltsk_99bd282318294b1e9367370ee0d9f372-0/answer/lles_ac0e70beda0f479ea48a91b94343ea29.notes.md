# TOPIC: POPULATION SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Meaning of population and census

### Meaning of population
**Population** is the total number of people living in an area at a given time. People may live in villages, towns, districts or an entire country. **Population distribution** is the way people are spread over an area. **Population growth** means an increase in the number of people in an area. **Population density** means the number of people living in an area per square kilometre. **Population structure** describes the population in terms of age and sex. These terms help explain the size and characteristics of groups of people.

### Population census and enumerators
A **population census** is the official counting of people in a country and the gathering of information about them. The people who collect census information are called **enumerators**. The notes identify the **Uganda Bureau of Statistics (UBOS)** as the body responsible for conducting the census. The Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development is named as the government ministry involved. **Census night** is the night before the counting exercise begins. A census is different from an ordinary population estimate because it is an organised national exercise.
<!-- source: lchk_401b8346cdcf44109b333a534b4a6b3d "POPULATION CENSUS" -->

### Why a census is carried out
A population census provides information that helps government plan for the people. The notes give other reasons: knowing how many people live in the country, understanding the age and sex structure of the population and determining birth and death rates. Information from a census also helps planners understand education levels and living standards. A census is costly to organise, so the source notes describe a ten-year interval that allows population changes to take place. The purpose is to gather useful information for planning public needs.
<!-- source: lchk_401b8346cdcf44109b333a534b4a6b3d "Importance of carrying out a population census in a country." -->

### Information collected during a census
Enumerators collect different kinds of information about households. The source lists the ages and sex of family members, numbers of children, occupation, religion, type of housing, main source of household energy and level of education. These details help describe the population. Census work may face challenges such as bad weather, difficult transport routes, insecurity and people who do not understand its purpose. By counting people and collecting their information, the census gives planners a clearer picture of the communities that need services.
