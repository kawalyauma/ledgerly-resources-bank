# TOPIC: THE GOVERNMENT OF UGANDA
## SUB-TOPIC: Children's rights, child abuse and child labour

### Children's rights
**Children's rights** are the natural freedoms and protections that children should enjoy. The notes describe a **child** as a person below eighteen years of age. Examples of children's rights include the right to food, education, medical care, protection, play and a name. These rights help children grow, learn and remain safe. Protecting the rights of children promotes their welfare and reduces harm. Families, communities and public institutions have responsibilities for ensuring that children are protected.
<!-- source: lchk_ed2ae9295c774104940e12d5d76c170c "CHILDREN’S RIGHTS" -->

### Meaning and forms of child abuse
**Child abuse** is the violation of children's rights. The source notes name physical, emotional and psychological child abuse. They also describe practices such as neglect, denial of food, forced early marriage and harmful treatment of children. Poverty, family problems, war and neglect are named among causes of child abuse. Abuse can cause injuries, poor health and interruption of schooling. Children need safe homes, education and support from responsible adults. Abuse can be reduced by teaching people about children's rights and reporting harmful behaviour to responsible authorities.
<!-- source: lchk_ed2ae9295c774104940e12d5d76c170c "CHILD ABUSE" -->

### Child labour and its dangers
**Child labour** is the employment of children in work that may be heavy or harmful. The notes give examples such as carrying heavy loads, working at building sites, working in mines and doing excessive domestic work. Reasons identified include poverty, child neglect and demand for cheap labour. The dangers include injury, illness, stunted growth and dropping out of school. Child labour can deprive children of opportunities to learn and enjoy their rights. Children should not be made to do harmful work at the expense of their health or education.
<!-- source: lchk_6dd64f60202a49f7807c72cd6775b286 "CHILD LABOUR" -->

### Ways of protecting children
The sources suggest several ways to reduce child abuse and harmful child labour. These include teaching children and adults about children's rights, enforcing laws against abuse, providing formal education and helping families reduce poverty. Cases of abuse should be reported to responsible adults or authorities. Encouraging adults to care properly for children also supports child protection. Child welfare depends on access to essential needs such as food, care, safety and schooling. Protecting children helps them grow and participate in school and family life.
