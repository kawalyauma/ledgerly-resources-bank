# TOPIC: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Practice: high population density

### Remember
1. High population density means **many people** compared with an area's size or resources.
2. Many people provide a **large market** for goods and can supply workers for industries.
3. More people may also increase tax collection and promote development.
4. An area with many people can experience **unemployment**, disease spread and food shortages.
5. Other problems include **crime**, traffic jams, environmental degradation and **slum development**.
6. **Land fragmentation** means dividing land into smaller plots.
7. Storeyed housing, improved rural services and more industries are suggested solutions.
8. Family planning and education are named among the ways to control rapid population growth.

### Crowded settlements and their problems
When many people need housing, land may be divided into small plots. The supplied materials call this **land fragmentation**. They also link rapid population growth with developing slums and increasing demand for social services. A shortage of jobs can lead to unemployment, while crowded living conditions can encourage communicable diseases to spread. Overuse of land and other natural resources can damage the environment. These issues show why planning and services are important in areas where many people live.
<!-- source: lchk_401b8346cdcf44109b333a534b4a6b3d "It leads to shortage of land for settlement leading to land fragmentation." -->

### What the benefits and problems show
A population can provide workers, customers and taxes, but the same large population can also put pressure on housing, sanitation, schools and food supplies. The source materials therefore consider the **advantages and disadvantages** together. They identify people’s access to work and social services as important when studying living conditions. The exercise below uses actual questions from the learning materials to revise these points.
