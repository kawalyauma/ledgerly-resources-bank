# TOPIC: THE GOVERNMENT OF UGANDA
## SUB-TOPIC: Communication

### Meaning of communication
**Communication** is the sending and receiving of messages. It helps people exchange information. Communication can take place between people nearby or over long distances. The notes explain **mass media** as sending messages to many people at the same time. Radios, televisions and newspapers are examples of mass media. Government uses communication as one of the public services provided to citizens. Effective communication is important when people need information about services, trade and matters affecting the community.

### Traditional and modern means
The sources name several **traditional means of communication**: drums, clapping hands, horns, ululation, smoke signals, whistles and special messengers. These methods have been used to pass information from one person or community to another. **Modern means of communication** include radio, television, telephones, newspapers, letters, magazines and billboards. Some modern methods can reach many people, while others are used for messages between individuals. Both traditional and modern forms serve the same general purpose of passing information from a sender to a receiver.
<!-- source: lchk_3b379eae4956473980abd2ec5698fb8d "Traditional ways of communication." -->

### Importance of communication
Communication is important to the development of society. The notes list promotion of **trade**, **national unity** and **peace** as important benefits. Buyers and sellers use communication to share information that helps trade take place. People learn about events and services through communication. Messages about public programmes can also reach members of communities. Communication supports sharing information across different parts of Uganda. Mass media such as radio, television and newspapers can be used to communicate with large numbers of people at once.
<!-- source: lchk_3b379eae4956473980abd2ec5698fb8d "Importance of communication." -->

### Barriers and possible solutions
Effective communication may be made difficult by **poor network coverage**, **illiteracy**, **poverty**, poor transport networks and high costs of communication. The notes also identify high taxes on communication services among the barriers. Suggested solutions include providing education, reducing communication costs, improving transport networks and regulating communication companies. The source also suggests a common national language to reduce language barriers. Addressing these problems can make it easier for people in different areas to send and receive information. Uganda's media services are regulated by the Uganda Communications Commission (UCC).
