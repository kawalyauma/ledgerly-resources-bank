# TOPIC: THE GOVERNMENT OF UGANDA
## SUB-TOPIC: The 1995 constitution, functions of a constitution and its relationship with elections

### Making the 1995 Constitution
The **1995 Constitution** was prepared after views were collected from citizens across Uganda. The **Constitutional Commission** gathered these views and worked on a draft. The **Constituent Assembly Delegates (CADs)** were elected in 1994 and debated and finalised the Constitution. They were representatives given the task of making or changing the constitution. The source notes say that the assembly's debate and writing took sixteen months. The new Constitution was made public on **8 October 1995** at Constitutional Square in Kampala, formerly known as City Square.

### People involved and their duties
Several people are named in the historical notes on constitution-making.
1. **Justice Benjamin Odoki** chaired the Constitutional Commission that collected citizens' views.
2. **Stephen Akabway** chaired the Interim Electoral Commission that organised elections of Constituent Assembly Delegates.
3. **James Wapakhabulo** chaired the Constituent Assembly as it debated the proposed constitution.
4. **Professor Victoria Mwaka** served as Deputy Chairperson of the Constituent Assembly.
These are historical roles in the preparation of the 1995 Constitution. The constituent assembly used the work of the commission to debate and complete a national constitution.

### Functions and importance
A **national constitution** lays down the basic laws of the country and guides how government should operate. Its important functions include protecting the rights of citizens, supporting democracy, encouraging national unity and providing ways to settle conflicts peacefully. It helps a country change its leadership peacefully. It also identifies the responsibilities of the organs of government. The constitution gives leaders and citizens rules they can refer to when public questions arise. The 1995 Constitution is described as the people's Constitution because views were collected from Ugandans.

### The constitution and elections
The **constitution** and **elections** are connected because elections are held according to legal rules. The constitution provides the duration for holding elections. It sets conditions for people who want to stand as candidates, as well as conditions for people who take part in elections. It also states the functions of the Electoral Commission. Elections are therefore part of constitutional governance rather than activities without rules. These arrangements support regular selection of leaders and peaceful transitions. The Electoral Commission carries out elections under the laws governing the country.
