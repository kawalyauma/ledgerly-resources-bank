# TOPIC: THE GOVERNMENT OF UGANDA
## SUB-TOPIC: The constitution: meaning and Uganda's constitutions since independence

### Meaning of a constitution
A **constitution** is a set of laws by which a country is governed. The **national constitution** is the supreme law of Uganda. It lays down rules for government and provides a basis for the laws that guide the country. The constitution also describes the duties of institutions and helps to protect citizens' rights. A constitution for a country concerns the nation as a whole. **By-laws**, on the other hand, are laws made by the local government or local councils. They apply to local matters under those councils.

### Constitutions since independence
Uganda has had different national constitutions since independence. The supplied P5 notes list the **1962 Constitution**, also called the Independence Constitution, which led Uganda to independence. The **1966 Constitution** was known as the Pigeon-hole Constitution. It came after a conflict between the country's political leaders and was not discussed by Parliament in the normal way. The **1967 Constitution** was called the Republican Constitution. It was later replaced by the **1995 Constitution**, which was based on views gathered from Ugandans and was made public in October 1995. These are four major constitutions named in the source notes.

### Changes under the 1967 Constitution
The **1967 Constitution** changed the way Uganda was governed. The source notes state that it abolished kingdoms and declared Uganda a republic. The President was given greater powers to govern the country than the Prime Minister. The 1967 Constitution is therefore commonly called the **Republican Constitution**. Some other notes mention that more powers were concentrated in central government and that political freedoms were limited. These were major differences from the earlier constitution. The term **republic** refers to a state with a President as head of state under its laws.

### Importance of a constitution
A constitution helps a country establish the responsibilities of its organs of government. It supports the protection of the rights of citizens, peaceful changes in leadership and democratic government. It provides a way to settle disputes according to law. It also guides national policies and administration. Uganda's different constitutions show that constitutional arrangements can change over time. The national constitution remains the country's highest law, while by-laws are local rules. Both are relevant to the way different levels of government organise society.
