# TOPIC: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Ways of controlling population growth

### Population growth
**Population growth** is an increase in the number of people living in an area. The resource pack identifies high fertility, early marriages, improved medical services and unplanned family sizes among factors that can increase population. The size of the population affects the demand for food, homes, school places, medical services and employment. A rapid increase may create difficulties when available resources and services are not sufficient.

### Problems connected with rapid growth
The supplied materials associate fast population growth with **food scarcity**, **housing shortages**, **land fragmentation**, **unemployment**, crime, environmental damage and the spread of communicable diseases. A growing population can increase the demand for schools, health services and transport. The resources also identify reduced access to available resources as a concern. These problems provide reasons for planning the size and needs of a population.

### Ways of controlling population growth
The sources list **family planning campaigns**, **education for girls**, discouraging **early marriages** and discouraging **polygamy**. Educating families about the cost of raising many children is another measure. Some passages describe tax benefits for small families. Public information campaigns can help people understand the advantages of a smaller, planned family size. The sources present these as ways of managing rapid population increase.

### Family planning and services
**Family planning** is described in the pack as spacing the births of children. It can help families plan for children and provide basic needs. The texts also propose increasing agricultural food production and improving the provision of social and medical services for communities with growing populations. These are measures for meeting needs as well as slowing growth. Together they relate population planning to family welfare and national development.
