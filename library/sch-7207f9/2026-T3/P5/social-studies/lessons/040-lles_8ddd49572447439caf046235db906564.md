# TOPIC: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Low population density: advantages, disadvantages and solutions

### Meaning and causes
**Low population density**, also called **sparse population**, means few people occupy an area compared with the available land or resources. The materials name **semi-arid areas**, forests, steep slopes and areas affected by war or natural disasters as sparsely populated places. Unreliable rainfall, infertile soil, poor social services, lack of jobs and insecurity are among the reasons that people may avoid settling in some areas. Population is therefore not spread equally everywhere.

### Advantages of low population density
With fewer residents, people may have **enough land** and more available accommodation. Crime rates may be low, while a smaller population can put less pressure on the natural environment. The sources state that this situation can promote **conservation** of the environment. Widely separated households may also experience less rapid spread of communicable diseases. Another source connects low population with easier planning and provision of services for the smaller population.

### Disadvantages of low population density
When few people live in an area there may be a **small market** for products. Industries, farms and businesses can face a **shortage of workers**. The government may receive **less tax revenue** from the area. Natural and economic resources can remain **underused**, and social services may be poor or limited. A low number of customers and workers can discourage economic activities. These points are found in several of the supplied population resources.

### Ways to improve settlement
The supplied resources propose **better social services** and **setting up industries** to attract people and create employment. They also mention mechanisation of agriculture as a way to increase food production. Improving living conditions in areas with few people can make those places more attractive for settlement. The causes of sparse settlement should also be understood: for example, poor rainfall, insecurity and poor soils may discourage people even when land is available.
