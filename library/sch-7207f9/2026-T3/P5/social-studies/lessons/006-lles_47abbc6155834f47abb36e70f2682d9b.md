# TOPIC: THE GOVERNMENT OF UGANDA
## SUB-TOPIC: The Legislature: meaning, composition and duties

### Meaning of the Legislature
The **Legislature** is the law-making organ of government. It is also called **Parliament** or the **National Assembly**. Its main responsibility is to make and amend laws that people should obey. The Legislature is headed by the **Speaker of Parliament**. Parliament is different from the Executive, which implements government policies, and the Judiciary, which interprets the laws. The work of Parliament helps establish rules for the country and provides checks on how government carries out its responsibilities.

### Members of Parliament
The Legislature is made up of **Members of Parliament (MPs)**. Some MPs are directly elected to represent constituencies, which are electoral areas. There are also women representatives from districts and members representing special interest groups. These groups include the youth, workers, the army, women and people with disabilities. The notes also describe **ex-officio members** as ministers without constituencies who may sit in Parliament without voting. Members come from different groups so that national law-making includes different kinds of representation.

### How Parliament performs its duties
The Legislature has several duties in the country.
1. It makes new national laws and amends existing laws.
2. It approves the national budget.
3. It checks how public money is spent.
4. It approves senior government officials appointed by the President.
5. It considers changes to the national constitution when needed.
6. It may discipline ministers and other public officials for misconduct.
Through these responsibilities, Parliament takes part in national leadership and oversight as well as making laws. The budget and expenditure duties concern public money and its use for the country's work.

### Relationship with other organs
The **Executive**, **Legislature** and **Judiciary** perform related but different functions. Parliament makes the laws. The Judiciary uses these laws when deciding cases and punishing law breakers, while the Executive administers government programmes. The three arms therefore contribute to government in distinct ways. Parliament's law-making duty is especially important because the courts need laws to interpret, and government officials must operate under the laws of the country. In the colonial period, the law-making institution was known as the Legislative Council, or LEGCO.
