# TOPIC: THE GOVERNMENT OF UGANDA
## SUB-TOPIC: Citizenship and responsibilities of citizens

### Meaning of citizenship
**Citizenship** is the legal right of belonging to a country. A **citizen** is a person legally recognised as belonging to a country. The source notes explain that citizenship may be obtained by birth, descent, registration, naturalisation or adoption. A person whose parents are Ugandan citizens may acquire citizenship by birth. Citizenship by descent is linked to Ugandan ancestors. Registration and naturalisation are other legal processes through which a person may become a citizen. Citizenship is important because it establishes a person's recognised connection to a country.

### Single and dual citizenship
The notes describe **single citizenship** as belonging legally to one country, and **dual citizenship** as belonging legally to more than one country. Citizenship may be lost in some circumstances described in the learning materials. These include acquiring it fraudulently, spying against one's country or joining an enemy country's armed forces. Citizenship should therefore be understood as a legal status with accompanying duties. Being a citizen does not merely mean living in a place; a person must have the legal right to belong to that country.

### Responsibilities of a citizen
A **good citizen** has duties and responsibilities to the country and the community. The source notes list these duties:
1. Obey the laws and help maintain law and order.
2. Pay taxes when required by law.
3. Respect the rights and freedoms of others.
4. Take part in useful community work.
5. Protect public property and care for the environment.
6. Help defend the country and support peace.
7. Participate in public activities that strengthen democracy.
These responsibilities help the community live together in peace and support development.

### Citizenship documents and public services
A **passport** is an official document issued to citizens for international travel. A **national identity card** is a legal document used to identify a citizen. The source notes explain that the national ID can also help people access public services. Identification documents therefore help show a person's citizenship status when required. Citizens can use services provided by the state while also carrying out their legal and community responsibilities. Citizenship is connected with rights, duties and recognition as a member of the country.
