# TOPIC: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: HIV/AIDS: spread, prevention and care

### Meaning of HIV and AIDS
**HIV** stands for **Human Immunodeficiency Virus**. **AIDS** stands for **Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome**. The supplied Social Studies text describes care for people affected by AIDS and the organisation providing support in Uganda. HIV and AIDS are important when studying population because illness can affect families, work and the number of people in a community. The materials describe different ways infection can spread and ways of reducing risk.

### Ways HIV can spread
The pack identifies sharing **sharp objects** with an infected person, contact between an open wound and infected blood, and receiving infected blood through a transfusion as ways infection can spread. It also names **unprotected sexual intercourse** with an infected person and use of **unsterilised sharp instruments** during some cultural practices. These sources show the importance of protecting people from contact with infected blood and from other transmission risks.

### Prevention
The resource describes an **ABC** message for prevention: **A** is abstaining from sex, **B** is being faithful to a partner and **C** is condom use. It also identifies shared sharp objects and unsterilised instruments as transmission risks. These are ways the textbook presents prevention. The lesson uses the age-appropriate message that sharp instruments should not be shared and that people should protect themselves from infected blood.

### Care and support
In Uganda, the source names **The AIDS Support Organisation (TASO)** as an organisation supporting people affected by AIDS. TASO is described as providing **counselling**, **antiretroviral drugs (ARVs)** and **food relief**. The supplied text also discusses effects of illness on people and families. Care and support are important for people who are living with illness and for the family members who depend on them.
