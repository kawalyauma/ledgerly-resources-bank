# TOPIC: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Population terms

### Meaning of population and density
**Population** is the total number of people living in an area. **Population density** is the number of people living per square kilometre. **Population distribution** is the way people are spread across an area. Some areas have many residents and others have few. These terms help describe how many people live in a place and how they are settled on the land.

### Population compared with resources
**Optimum population** is when the available resources can support the number of people. **Underpopulation** means fewer people than the available resources could support. **Overpopulation** means more people than the available resources can support. The number of people should therefore be compared with the land, employment and other resources available. Population size alone does not show whether an area has enough resources.

### Growth and structure
**Population growth** is the increase in the number of people living in an area. **Population explosion** is a sudden increase in the number of people. **Population structure** is the composition of a population in terms of age and sex. Growth describes change in population numbers; structure describes the kinds of people making up the population. These definitions are part of the pack's population terminology summary.

### Census and distribution
**Population census** is the general counting of people in an area. **Census night** is the night before counting takes place. A **dense population** has many people compared with land area, while a **sparse population** has few people compared with land area. A census can provide information about the population size, age and sex of people, which the government uses for planning and provision of social services.
