# TOPIC: POPULATION SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Population growth and the factors that influence it

### Meaning of population growth
**Population growth** means an increase in the number of people living in a place over time. The place can be a village, district or country. **Population explosion** means a sudden or rapid increase in the number of people living in an area. The size of a population does not always remain the same. It can change as people are born, die or move from one place to another. Studying population growth helps explain changing needs in communities and the number of people needing services.

### Births, deaths and movement
The number of **births** affects the rate at which a population grows. A high birth rate tends to increase the population. Deaths reduce population numbers. The source notes also name **migration** as a factor: when people move into an area, its population may increase, while moving out may reduce it. A large number of refugees arriving in an area can cause a sudden change. These factors explain how population growth may differ between one community and another. Births, deaths and migration are therefore important to population studies.

### Factors that increase population
The supplied notes identify conditions that may encourage population growth. These include increased food production, better medical services, improved security and high fertility rates. Early marriage and polygamous marriage are also listed. When people have enough food and better health care, more may survive. Peace can make families remain in an area, while migration may bring new people. These factors can act together and cause communities to grow at different rates. Population growth reflects both changes in families and wider social conditions.

### Population growth and services
As the number of people rises, more people may need houses, school places, health care, land and jobs. A larger population may also provide more workers and buyers of goods. The source notes explain that the government collects more taxes from areas with large populations. It can use census information to plan for new needs. Population growth can have positive and negative effects, depending on whether services and resources are sufficient. Understanding the causes of growth helps in discussing those effects.
