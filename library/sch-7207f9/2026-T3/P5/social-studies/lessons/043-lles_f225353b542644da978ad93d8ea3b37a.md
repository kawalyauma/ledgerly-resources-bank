# TOPIC: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Social and economic effects of HIV/AIDS on the population

### Meaning of social effects
**Social effects** are the consequences of illness for people, their households and communities. The source discusses HIV/AIDS as part of population studies. It identifies death, hardship and family changes among the effects of AIDS. The number of people living in a community can change when people die. Affected families can also experience difficulties in caring for children and meeting everyday needs.

### Social effects on families
The source lists **death of people**, **misery for an affected person**, **isolation** and an increase in the number of **orphans** following the deaths of parents. These are social consequences because they involve families, relationships and the wellbeing of individuals. Orphaned children may lose parental support. The same resource also identifies an organisation offering support. Care and counselling are part of the response described in the pack.

### Economic effects on work
**Economic effects** concern work, production, income and trade. The source says AIDS can lead to **loss of efficiency at work**, a **smaller labour supply** through deaths and a **reduced market for goods**. When fewer people are able to work or buy goods, economic activity can be affected. The materials also connect illness with poverty in families when money is spent on treatment.

### The link between population and production
Population size affects the number of workers and customers in an area. The resource pack connects a large population with a supply of labour and a market for goods. It then explains that death and illness associated with HIV/AIDS can reduce both labour and market activity. This helps explain why effects on health can also become effects on economic production and family income. TASO's counselling, medicines and food support are the forms of assistance specifically identified in the materials.
