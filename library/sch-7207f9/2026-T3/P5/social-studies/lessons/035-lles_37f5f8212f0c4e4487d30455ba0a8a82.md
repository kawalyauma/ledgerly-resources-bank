# TOPIC: POPULATION SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION
## SUB-TOPIC: Population density

### Meaning of population density
**Population density** is the average number of people living in an area per square kilometre. It relates the number of people to the size of the land they occupy. Density is useful for comparing areas of different sizes. A large area may have many residents but low density when the people are spread widely. A smaller area may have higher density if it contains many residents in a small space. The population-density measure uses people per square kilometre as its unit.

### How density is calculated
The source notes use the relationship **population density = total population ÷ area in square kilometres**. To determine a place's density, both the total number of people and the land area must be known. The area is measured in square kilometres, written as km², while the resulting population density is expressed as people per km². Dividing the number of people by the area makes it possible to compare settlement levels. The source gives examples of this calculation using a population and an area. The formula is based on the number of people and the size of their living area.

### High and low population density
**High population density** means a relatively large number of people living in an area compared with its land size. Areas with good services, jobs and transport may have many residents living close together. **Low population density** means fewer people compared with the available area. It can be associated with rural places, poor services or difficult environmental conditions. Population density is not exactly the same as the overall number of inhabitants; it is the number relative to land area.

### Difference between population terms
**Population size** is the number of people living in a particular area. **Population distribution** describes how those people are spread across different places. **Population growth** refers to the increase in their number over time. **Population density** is the number per square kilometre. These terms answer different questions: how many people live there, where they live, how their number changes, and how concentrated the settlement is. When planning social services, government may need information about population size, distribution and density.
