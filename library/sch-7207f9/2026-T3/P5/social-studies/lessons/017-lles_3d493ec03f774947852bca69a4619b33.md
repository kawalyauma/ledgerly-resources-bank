# TOPIC: THE GOVERNMENT OF UGANDA
## SUB-TOPIC: Sources of government revenue and taxation

### Meaning and sources of revenue
**Government revenue** means income earned or collected by government from different sources. The notes identify taxation as the major source of government revenue in Uganda. Revenue helps government to carry out its programmes. Other sources include court fines, grants, loans, donations, tourism, licence fees, agricultural exports and sale of minerals or electricity. Government income may come from businesses, services and support from other countries or institutions. The sources are not all the same: for example, loans must be paid back, while donations do not have to be repaid.

### Taxation and why governments collect taxes
**Taxation** is a system through which a government imposes and collects taxes. A **tax** is money paid to government by people and organisations to help provide public services. Tax income supports roads, schools, health care and payment of civil servants. Taxes are collected from different economic activities and incomes. The source notes identify two main groups of taxes: **direct taxes** and **indirect taxes**. Both are ways in which the government raises money to provide services and maintain administration.

### Direct taxes
A **direct tax** is collected directly from the person who is required to pay it. The supplied notes give **income tax**, **Pay As You Earn (PAYE)** and **property tax** as examples. Income tax is based on the money a person earns. PAYE is an income tax that is deducted from the earnings of workers. Property tax is collected on qualifying commercial buildings owned by people. Direct taxes differ from taxes included in prices of the things consumers buy. Both kinds provide revenue for government work.

### Indirect taxes
An **indirect tax** is paid by consumers through goods or services they buy. **Value Added Tax (VAT)** is charged on goods and services. **Customs duty**, also called import tax, applies to goods brought into the country. **Excise duty** applies to goods manufactured within the country. Traders may pass these tax costs to buyers through the prices of goods. Direct and indirect taxes both contribute to government revenue, but they are collected in different ways. Taxes remain a major source of funds for public services.
