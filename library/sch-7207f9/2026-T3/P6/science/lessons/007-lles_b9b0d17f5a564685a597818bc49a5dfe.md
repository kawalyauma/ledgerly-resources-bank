# TOPIC: SCIENCE AT HOME AND IN OUR COMMUNITY
## SUB-TOPIC: Decanting water

### Decanting
- **Decanting** is a method of removing large particles of objects from water.
- Water is poured slowly from one container to another.
- Heavy particles are left behind in the first container.
- The water is poured carefully so particles are not carried over.
- Decanting helps remove large particles from water.
- Decanting does not remove every impurity from water.
- Water impurities are contaminants or foreign objects in water.
- Water impurities make water unsafe for use.
- Impurities may be soluble or insoluble.
- Inorganic impurities consist of dissolved mineral salts.
- Organic impurities include bacteria, fungi and protozoa.
- Dead plant materials such as leaves and grass are organic impurities.

### System used in decanting
- The three pot system is used to purify water by decanting.
- Water is poured slowly between containers in the system.
- Heavy particles remain behind as water is poured.
- Decanting removes large particles from water.

### Examples of water impurities
- Human wastes
- Animal wastes like urine and dung
- Herbicides
- Insecticides
- Silt from erosion
- Leaves and grass
- Dissolved mineral salts
- Bacteria
- Fungi
- Protozoa

### Ways of preparing safe water
- Boiling water kills germs at 100°C (212°F).
- Filtering removes solid impurities such as dirt, soil and stones.
- Filtered water may still contain germs.
- Treatment adds chemicals to water to kill germs.
- Chemicals used include chlorine, water guard and aquasafe.
- Decanting removes large particles from water.
