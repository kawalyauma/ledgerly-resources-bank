# TOPIC: ACCIDENTS AND FIRST AID
## SUB-TOPIC: Burns and scalds

### Burns and scalds
- **Burns** are injuries caused by dry heat.
- Burns are body injuries caused by heat.
- A burn may be an injury on the skin caused by dry heat.
- **Scalds** are named as a type of burn injury.
- Burns and scalds are described by degree.
- Degree describes how severe a burn is.

### Causes of burns
- Hot metals
- Hot flat iron
- Acids
- Glowing charcoal
- Hot charcoal stove
- Fire
- Hot plates
- Cookers
- Un-insulated electric wires carrying current
- Chemicals like acids

### Degrees of burns
- There are three degrees of burns.
- First degree burns are minor burns which do not form blisters.
- A blister is raised skin with liquid underneath.
- The skin may be tender for several days.
- The skin is unbroken in first degree burns.
- Second degree burns form blisters.
- Second degree burns are more severe than first degree burns.
- Third degree burns are the most severe burns.
- Third degree burns burn the skin deeply.
- Third degree burns may appear shiny white.

### First aid for burns
- Put a first degree burn in cool water immediately after the accident.
- Cool water reduces the temperature of the burnt part.
- First degree burns need no dressing.
- If a second degree blister is broken, wash the area with clean water and soap.
- Cover the skin with a bandage or clean cloth.
- It is not good to break a blister.
- Breaking a blister may lead to wound infection by germs.
- Fats, oil, coffee, herbs or dung should never be put on a burn.
- These substances can cause infection.

### Note
- Degrees describe how severe burns are.
- First degree burns do not form blisters.
- Second degree burns form blisters.
- Third degree burns are the most severe.
