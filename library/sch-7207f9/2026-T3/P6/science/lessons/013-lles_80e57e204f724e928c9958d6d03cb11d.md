# TOPIC: ACCIDENTS AND FIRST AID
## SUB-TOPIC: First aid for burns

### First aid for burns
- Put the burnt area in cool water.
- If the blister is not broken, leave it.
- Leaving an unbroken blister prevents infection of the wound.
- If the blister is broken, wash it with soap and clean water.
- Cover broken skin with cloth to prevent flies bringing germs.
- Encourage a casualty with a third degree burn to drink fluids.
- Second and third degree burn patients should drink plenty of fluids.
- Burnt skin loses fluids through evaporation.
- Cool water helps reduce the temperature of the injured part.
- Cool water prevents further damage to underlying body cells.
- Sugar attracts houseflies which bring germs to a wound.

### First aid for scalds
- A scald is an injury caused by wet heat.
- A scald is an injury caused by hot liquids.
- Put the injured part in cold water for 10–15 minutes.
- Pour cold water on the injured part if it cannot be dipped.

### Prevention of burns and scalds
- Use heat insulators to handle hot objects.
- Keep hot objects far from children’s reach.
- Cook food in raised fireplaces.
- Keep children from playing near fireplaces.
- Keep petroleum products out of children’s reach.
- Refill lamps after putting them off.
- Keep children from playing with hot liquids and metals.
- Keep inflammable liquids such as petrol away from living houses.
- Construct fire guards around cooking places.
- Avoid appliances with uninsulated electric wires.
- Teach children the dangers of burns.

### Note
- Cool water is used on burnt and scalded areas.
- Do not break an unbroken blister.
- Second and third degree burn patients need plenty of fluids.
