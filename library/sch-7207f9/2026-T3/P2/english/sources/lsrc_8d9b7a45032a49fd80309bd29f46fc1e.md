Read the passage below and answer the questions that follow it.

According to Dr. Jimmy Opigo, the Assistant commissioner health

services at the Health Ministry, the main focus this year is on individual action.
He says malaria has four dimensions.
Fighting the mosquito, fighting the parasites, modifying the environment,
adjusting human behaviour, practices and choices. ‘This year we want people to
understand that fighting malaria starts with them before the government comes
in support where they have failed. People should not let themselves be bitten by
mosquitoes’, he said.

Malaria is a life threatening disease caused by parasites that are
transmitted to people through the bites of infected female anopheles mosquitoes.
It is preventable and curable. The World Health Organisation (WHO) says
Africa carries a disproportionately high share of the global malaria burden.

Statistics from the health ministry show that malaria is still ripe in
Uganda, accounting for over 19% of the deaths. The statistics also show that
malaria’s incidence is 478 cases per 1,000 of the population per year.

According to Opigo, malaria has negative economic effects, for the
national economy. It leads to low production. At the household level, it causes
an immense burden, particularly for poor households. It reduces the days a
patient can work and adds costs relating to care. Malaria takes over 30% of
household income. Treating one malaria case in Uganda costs between Shs.
50,000 and Shs.100,000. It also causes loss of productivity for about three days.
Malaria leads to loss of school time because it keeps the child and teacher at
home while others are studying or teaching.

Forty percent of school absenteeism is caused by malaria. Malaria also puts a
heavy burden on the health system, accounting for up to 40% of all outpatient
visits.

Hajara Namirembe, a nurse and mid wife at Naguru Hospital in Kampala,
says malaria infections in pregnant women result in adverse pregnancy
outcomes including spontaneous abortions, neo-natal deaths and low birth
weight. Other complications during pregnancy are reduced neurocognitive
functions in the child, which lower educational attainment and depress literacy
rates in the long term.

According to Opigo, mosquitoes are becoming resistant to chemicals that
kill them. There is resurgence where progress had been made and this affects
sustainability. ‘Gaps still exist in building malaria resilience culture. Therefore,
there is still need for the government to continue sensitising the community
about how to control malaria and why they need to treat it as soon as they detect
it’, he explained.

(Adapted from: The New Vision April 25, 2019)
